Talking about Bursa in Indonesia

Created on November 19, 2026

2026   ·   plasma   ozone   collaboration   ·   talks

This year I got to talk about my work in Indonesia twice: once in person in Semarang in April, and once online for my old department at UMS in November. Both times I went with my supervisor, Prof. Dr. Kadir Çavdar.

Semarang, April 2026

On 15 April, Universitas Diponegoro (Undip) and Bursa Uludağ University signed a memorandum of understanding at Undip’s rectorate in Semarang. Rector Prof. Dr. Suharnomo received our delegation: Prof. Çavdar, Mehmet Öztürk (CEO of Mertay Makina), and me. The agreement covers joint research, joint supervision of master’s and doctoral students, and student exchange. Prof. Çavdar told the room that our side is ready to start joint research.

The next day, 16 April, Undip’s Faculty of Science and Mathematics and its Center for Plasma Research held a public lecture as part of the Indonesian Plasma Research Consortium (IPRC) week, 14 to 17 April. Prof. Çavdar opened with Applications of Atmospheric Plasma in Polymer Adhesion and Ozone Sterilization. He showed how plasma activation lets polypropylene car parts take paint without a primer layer, which is work he has carried from industry into the lab.

My talk was Bursa’s Industrial Heritage and Mertay Makina’s Engineering Journey. I wanted the audience to see Bursa the way I see it after several years there: a city with a long manufacturing history, a university with a technopark, and companies that actually work with researchers. Mertay Makina, where I design fixtures and mechanisms for automotive clients, is one example of that link between factory and campus.

UMS, November 2026

On 4 and 11 November, the Mechanical Engineering program at Universitas Muhammadiyah Surakarta (UMS), where I studied and later taught, invited us for two online guest lectures. In the first session I introduced Bursa, the university, and its manufacturing ecosystem, and Prof. Çavdar spoke about AI in computer-aided design and manufacturing. In the second, he talked about his career in industry and applied plasma research, and I presented part of my dissertation: a closed-loop ozone sterilizer for heat-sensitive medical equipment such as rubber, plastic, and dressings.
