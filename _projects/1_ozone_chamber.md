Reusable medical devices that cannot tolerate heat need a low-temperature sterilant. Ozone works well and leaves no residue, but whether every surface in a chamber receives enough of it depends on how the gas is generated, carried, and decays.

My dissertation, Mathematical Modeling, CFD Simulation, and Experimental Validation of Gas-Phase Ozone Transport in a Closed Recirculating Sterilization Chamber (supervisor: Prof. Dr. Kadir Çavdar), tackles this in three connected parts:

1. Mathematical model of ozone generation, transport, and decomposition kinetics in the chamber.
2. ANSYS Fluent CFD simulation of the recirculating flow and concentration field.
3. Experimental validation on a prototype chamber I designed, built, and instrumented for gas concentration and flow measurement.

Expected defense: December 2026. Two further papers from the dissertation are in preparation.
