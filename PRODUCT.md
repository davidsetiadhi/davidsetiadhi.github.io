# davestia.com: product brief

## Who the site is for
1. **Recruiters and HR staff abroad.** They have no engineering background. In under a minute they need to know who David is, what he works on, why it is useful, and whether he is available.
2. **Hiring managers in R&D and academia.** They want to see the scope of the work, David's own role in it, the skills behind it, and the papers.
3. **Researchers and possible collaborators.** They look for topics, the team, and a way to get in touch.

## What the home page must answer
- Who is this? The answer is David's face, his name, and one plain sentence about what he does.
- What does he work on, and why does it matter? The interactive research map answers this. Each topic says who benefits and where the work is going.
- Who does he work with? The team, shown with faces. Davestia Lab is David plus this team.
- Is he available? Yes: PhD defense in mid-December 2026, open to postdoc, lecturer and R&D roles from January 2027.

## Voice
- Plain English that a non-engineer understands on first reading. Short sentences.
- Say what a project is for and who it helps before saying how it is done.
- No slogans and no hype (revolutionary, cutting-edge, novel, pioneering). Do not chase publications for their own sake: show the benefit and the direction.
- No em dashes. Use one term per concept.

## Hard rules
- Unpublished work shows only the topic, the goal, the status and the people. It never shows numbers, results, materials, settings, or anything that is part of the novelty. The source is `_data/research.yml`, built from the project cards in `D:\Website\davestia\project-cards.yaml`.
- Funding: none to report.
- Do not publish a phone number. The email appears only through the obfuscating script.
- No device photos of the ozone prototype until Prof. Çavdar agrees. Concept sketches are fine.
- English only. Indonesian belongs to Instagram (the `/id/` page stays as it is).
